Demand runs far ahead of legal supply

On 20 October 2026, Reuters reported that ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent had placed orders for more than 3 million RTX PRO 5500 cards — over $35 billion at about $11,400 each. Across all Chinese buyers, orders passed 6 million chips. Nvidia's own answer was that buyers might receive 5% to 10% of what they asked for.

That one line holds the whole chapter. The demand is real and enormous, the supply that the rules allow is a sliver of it, and the space between the two is where every price, every grey-market deal, and every enforcement case in the later chapters lives.

Who is buying, and how much

An accelerator is the industry word for an AI chip: the processor that trains and runs large models. About 4 million accelerators shipped into China in 2025, of which 2.36 million came from Nvidia and AMD and roughly 1.65 million were Chinese-made.

The buyers are the big internet firms. ByteDance, Alibaba, Tencent and Baidu take most of the volume, with telecom carriers and state labs behind them. The spending is hard to pin down because the largest buyer, ByteDance, files nothing: its reported 2026 plan of about RMB 200 billion in capital spending, with roughly RMB 85 billion for AI chips, rests on unnamed sources and should be read as such. What is filed points the same way. Alibaba, Tencent and Baidu together reported RMB 131.9 billion (about US$19.4 billion) of capital spending in the June 2026 quarter, up 114% from a year earlier, though not all of that is chips — Alibaba says part is simply higher component prices.

The order books dwarf what can be shipped. In January 2026, Chinese firms had ordered more than 2 million H200s at about $27,000 each, against Nvidia stock of 700,000. The reader should treat these totals with care: our market team warns that firms double-order across suppliers to hedge, so headline orders overstate true need. Even discounted, they describe a market straining against a ceiling.

What the law lets through

A license is written approval from the US Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) to ship a named chip to a named buyer. For the top chips, almost none has flowed.

By May 2026, the US had licensed about ten Chinese buyers for up to 75,000 H200s each. By August, only about 20,000 had reached the mainland, while Nvidia held roughly 500,000 H200s built for China that it could not sell. The H200 is Nvidia's licensable data-center chip; the newer Blackwell line (B200, B300 and the rack systems built from them) sits under a presumption of denial, meaning BIS starts from "no" on any application. So the lawful top-chip channel in 2026 is one chip, a few buyers, and a trickle of units.

Nvidia's collapsing share tells the same story from the seller's side. Bernstein traces Nvidia's slice of China's AI chip market from 66% in 2024 to about 8% in 2026. Unit-share figures from IDC run higher because they count lower-end parts, so the two measures are not like for like; the finding is the direction, not a single number.

The gap

Bloomberg Intelligence puts 2026 domestic demand at about 4.2 million chips against 2.6 million of supply — a shortfall near 1.6 million estimated, mostly for lack of memory. This 4.2-against-2.6 figure is the most quoted in the market, and it is also second-hand: we never traced it to the original Bloomberg note, so carry it as the best available estimate, not a measured count.

At Chinese chip prices, our market team's rough arithmetic sizes the gap at RMB 130 to 175 billion, about US$18 to 25 billion estimated. That is our own calculation from indicative quotes, not a figure any source published.

The binding limit is not the logic chip but the memory beside it. HBM (high-bandwidth memory) is the stacked memory that feeds an AI chip its data, and the US controlled it in December 2024. China's foundry SMIC could cut more processor dies than it can pair with memory, so the shortage is an HBM shortage wearing a chip shortage's clothes.

The price gap

Prices show the strain better than any order total. A lawful, licensed H200 is quoted near its normal price — about $27,000 in Chinese order reports, against a global reference of roughly $32,000 (a reference figure, not an Nvidia price sheet). No large lawful premium shows in the press, though the US side adds a 25% import tariff somewhere in the chain.

The premium lives in the grey market, where banned parts change hands outside licensed channels. A server packed with eight B300s ran from RMB 4 million to over 8 million in six months. The RTX PRO 6000D workstation card doubled on the secondary market even after Beijing told buyers to stop. Domestic chips rose too, pulled up by the same HBM shortage: Huawei's Ascend 950PR climbed from about RMB 60,000 to over 80,000 during 2026, a rise of about a third. When the lawful part is scarce and the domestic part is memory-starved, every number moves up at once.

What the squeeze did to Nvidia

Nvidia reports China revenue by customer billing location, and the figure has fallen hard. China including Hong Kong was $7.88 billion of $96.2 billion, 8.2%, in the quarter to 26 July 2026 — down from 15% a year earlier and about 19% in fiscal 2025.

Period China incl. HK Total Share
Q2 FY27 (to 26 Jul 2026) $7.88B $96.2B 8.2%
H1 FY27 $12.43B $177.8B 7.0%
H1 FY26 $13.64B $90.8B 15.0%
FY2025 about $25.1B $130.5B about 19.2%
FY2026 about $19.7B about $215.9B about 9.1%

The last two rows come from an aggregator reading of Nvidia's annual filing, not the filing itself, so treat the full-year totals as approximate. One detail inside the latest quarter matters: Hopper data-center shipments to China were under 1% of data-center revenue, so most of that $7.88 billion is lower-end and non-data-center product, not the top chips everyone is queuing for.

Two causes drove the fall, and Nvidia's own words assign them. The US side came first: the April 2025 license requirement on the H20 forced a $4.5 billion inventory charge and knocked the quarter's gross margin from 71.3% down to 60.5%. Then Beijing's side held the door shut even after US licenses returned. The 10-Q says plainly that H200 sales "were restricted by the PRC government" and that Nvidia "has been unable to sell all the products for which we have licenses," and it books a further $0.4 billion H200 charge for excess inventory. Nvidia's guidance now assumes no China data-center compute revenue at all. No source splits the loss cleanly between Washington's rule and Beijing's discouragement; any split would be an estimate.

One widely repeated figure does not appear in the filing at all. The reported deal under which H20 licenses carried 15% of China sales to the US Treasury is press-only; it is not in the 10-Q, and no payment is confirmed. Treat it as unverified.

The domestic alternative, and why it cannot yet close the gap

If the lawful Nvidia channel is a trickle and the grey market is a lottery, the obvious answer is to buy Chinese. Beijing presses exactly that. Huawei is the leader by far, with about 812,000 Ascend chips shipped in 2025, followed by Alibaba's T-Head, Cambricon (its first profitable year, revenue up over 450%) and Baidu's Kunlunxin.

The chips are real but still behind. Huawei publishes no datasheet for the Ascend 910C, so its specs are reported, not official:

Chip FP16 compute (TFLOPS) Memory Bandwidth
Ascend 910C about 752 to 800 128 GB 3.2 TB/s
Nvidia H100 989 80 GB 3.35 TB/s
Nvidia H200 989 141 GB 4.8 TB/s
Nvidia H20 148 96 GB 4.0 TB/s

On paper a 910C is close to an H100 for raw compute and carries more memory. In practice DeepSeek's testing, reported second-hand, put it at about 60% of an H100 on real inference work. Huawei's answer to the single-chip gap is scale: its CloudMatrix 384 wires 384 chips together and beats an Nvidia GB200 rack on total compute and memory, at about 2.3 times the power per operation. China has cheaper electricity than chips, so that trade works there.

Two walls keep the domestic chips from filling the gap. The first is memory: HBM is the tightest limit, and a global shortage caps how many complete chips China can build regardless of foundry capacity. The second is software. Nvidia's CUDA has had nearly two decades and millions of developers; Huawei's CANN, open-sourced in August 2025, runs existing code through an adapter and lags each release. (A vendor claim of "over 95% CUDA compatibility" is unverified — do not rely on it.) The pull of CUDA is part of why a gaming-class card like the RTX PRO 5500, priced close to an Ascend 950PR, still drew orders in the millions.

What this adds up to

The arithmetic is stubborn. Demand near 4.2 million, legal supply near 2.6 million, a gap near 1.6 million estimated that neither a licensed H200 trickle nor a memory-starved domestic line can close in 2026. Prices double. Nvidia writes off billions and forecasts nothing from China. Buyers keep ordering six million chips they know they cannot all get.

A gap this wide and this profitable does not sit quietly. It pulls chips through side doors — the grey-market servers, the false end-user statements, the transshipment hubs. That pull, and what happens to the people caught in it, is the next chapter.

需求远远跑在合法供应前面

2026 年 10 月 20 日，Reuters 报道称，字节跳动、阿里巴巴和腾讯已下单超过 300 万张 RTX PRO 5500，按每张约 $11,400 计算，总额超过 350 亿美元。算上所有中国买家，订单已超过 600 万颗芯片。Nvidia 自己的回应是：买家最终可能只拿到所订数量的 5% 到 10%。

谁在买，买多少

加速器（accelerator）是业内对 AI 芯片的叫法，指训练和运行大模型的处理器。2025 年约有 400 万颗加速器运入中国，其中 236 万颗来自 Nvidia 和 AMD，约 165 万颗为国产。

买家是大型互联网公司。字节跳动、阿里巴巴、腾讯和百度拿走了大部分份额，其后是电信运营商和国家实验室。支出规模很难说准，因为最大的买家字节跳动不公开披露任何数据：媒体报道称其 2026 年计划资本开支约 2,000 亿元人民币，其中约 850 亿元用于 AI 芯片，但这一说法来自匿名消息源，应当按此理解。已披露的数据指向同一方向。阿里巴巴、腾讯和百度在 2026 年 6 月季度合计报告资本开支 1,319 亿元人民币（约 194 亿美元），同比增长 114%，但其中并非全是芯片——阿里巴巴表示，部分增长只是因为元器件涨价。

订单规模远超可交付的数量。2026 年 1 月，中国企业已订购超过 200 万颗 H200，每颗约 $27,000，而 Nvidia 的库存只有 70 万颗。读者应谨慎看待这些总数：本站市场研究提醒，企业会在多家供应商之间重复下单以对冲风险，因此公开的订单数字高估了真实需求。即便打了折扣，这些数字仍然描绘出一个顶着天花板硬挤的市场。

法律放行了多少

许可证是美国商务部工业与安全局（BIS）出具的书面批准，允许把指定芯片运给指定买家。对于顶级芯片，几乎没有放行。

截至 2026 年 5 月，美国已向约十家中国买家发放许可证，每家最多 75,000 颗 H200。到 8 月，只有约 20,000 颗运抵中国大陆，而 Nvidia 手里还压着约 50 万颗为中国生产却卖不出去的 H200。H200 是 Nvidia 可以申请许可证的数据中心芯片；更新的 Blackwell 系列（B200、B300 以及用它们搭建的整机柜系统）适用推定拒绝，即 BIS 对任何申请的起点都是“不批”。所以 2026 年合法的顶级芯片渠道就是：一款芯片、几家买家、涓涓细流般的出货量。

Nvidia 份额的急剧下滑从卖方一侧讲述了同一个故事。Bernstein 统计，Nvidia 在中国 AI 芯片市场的份额从 2024 年的 66% 降到 2026 年的约 8%。IDC 的出货量份额数字更高，因为它把低端产品也算了进去，所以两种口径不能直接对比；结论在于方向，而不是某个单一数字。

缺口

Bloomberg Intelligence 估计，2026 年国内需求约 420 万颗，而供应约 260 万颗——缺口接近 160 万颗 估算，主要原因是内存不足。“420 万对 260 万”是市场上引用最多的数字，但它也是二手的：我们始终没有追溯到 Bloomberg 的原始报告，因此只能把它当作现有最佳估算，而不是实测数据。

按国内芯片价格计算，本站市场研究的粗略测算把缺口规模定在 1,300 亿到 1,750 亿元人民币，约合 180 亿到 250 亿美元 估算。这是我们根据参考报价自行计算的结果，没有任何来源公布过这一数字。

真正卡脖子的不是逻辑芯片，而是旁边的内存。HBM（高带宽内存）是为 AI 芯片输送数据的堆叠式内存，美国于 2024 年 12 月将其纳入管制。中国的晶圆代工厂中芯国际能切出的处理器裸片，比能配上的内存还多，所以这场短缺本质上是 HBM 短缺，只是披着芯片短缺的外衣。

价格差

价格比任何订单总数都更能说明压力。一颗合法获许可的 H200 报价接近正常水平——中国订单报道中约 $27,000，全球参考价约 $32,000（这是参考数字，不是 Nvidia 的价目表）。媒体报道中看不到合法渠道有明显溢价，不过美国方面在链条中的某一环加征了 25% 的进口关税。

溢价出现在灰色市场，被禁的芯片在许可渠道之外易手。一台装有八颗 B300 的服务器，六个月内价格从 400 万元人民币涨到 800 万元以上。RTX PRO 6000D 工作站显卡在二手市场上价格翻倍，即便北京已经叫买家停手。国产芯片也在涨，同样是被 HBM 短缺推高：华为昇腾 950PR 在 2026 年间从约 6 万元人民币涨到 8 万元以上，涨幅约三分之一。当合法产品稀缺、国产产品又缺内存时，所有数字会一起往上走。

这场挤压对 Nvidia 造成了什么

Nvidia 按客户账单地址报告中国区收入，这一数字大幅下滑。在截至 2026 年 7 月 26 日的季度，中国（含香港）收入为 $96.2B 中的 $7.88B，占 8.2%——一年前为 15%，2025 财年约为 19%。

27 财年上半年 $12.43B $177.8B 7.0%

最后两行来自数据汇总商对 Nvidia 年报的解读，而不是年报本身，因此全年数字应视为近似值。最新季度里有一个细节值得注意：发往中国的 Hopper 数据中心产品不到数据中心收入的 1%，所以这 78.8 亿美元大部分是低端和非数据中心产品，而不是大家排队抢购的顶级芯片。

下滑有两个原因，Nvidia 自己的表述已经分清了。先是美国方面：2025 年 4 月对 H20 实施的许可证要求，迫使 Nvidia 计提 45 亿美元库存减值，并把当季毛利率从 71.3% 打到 60.5%。随后，即便美国恢复发放许可证，北京方面依然把门关着。10-Q 明确写道，H200 的销售“受到中国政府限制”，Nvidia“无法销售所有已获许可证的产品”，并为过剩库存再计提了 4 亿美元 H200 减值。Nvidia 目前的业绩指引假设来自中国的数据中心计算收入为零。没有任何来源把损失清楚地分摊到华盛顿的规则和北京的劝阻上；任何分摊都只是估算。

有一个流传很广的数字在年报里根本找不到。据报道，H20 许可证附带一项安排，要把中国销售额的 15% 上交美国财政部，但这只见于媒体报道；10-Q 里没有，也没有任何付款得到确认。应视为未经证实。

国产替代，以及为什么它暂时补不上缺口

如果合法的 Nvidia 渠道只是细流，灰色市场又像抽奖，那么显而易见的答案就是买国产。北京正是这样推动的。华为遥遥领先，2025 年出货约 81.2 万颗昇腾芯片，其后是阿里巴巴的平头哥、寒武纪（首个盈利年度，收入增长超过 450%）和百度的昆仑芯。

这些芯片是真实可用的，但仍然落后。华为没有公布昇腾 910C 的规格书，所以以下参数来自报道，并非官方：

芯片 FP16 算力（TFLOPS） 内存 带宽
昇腾 910C 约 752 至 800 128 GB 3.2 TB/s
Nvidia H100 989 80 GB 3.35 TB/s
Nvidia H200 989 141 GB 4.8 TB/s
Nvidia H20 148 96 GB 4.0 TB/s

纸面上，910C 的原始算力接近 H100，内存还更大。但实际上，据二手报道，DeepSeek 的测试显示它在真实推理任务中只有 H100 的约 60%。华为对单芯片差距的回答是堆规模：它的 CloudMatrix 384 把 384 颗芯片连在一起，在总算力和总内存上超过 Nvidia 的 GB200 机柜，代价是每次运算功耗约为 2.3 倍。在中国，电比芯片便宜，所以这笔交换划得来。

有两堵墙挡着国产芯片填补缺口。第一堵是内存：HBM 是最紧的约束，全球短缺限制了中国能造出多少颗完整芯片，与代工产能无关。第二堵是软件。Nvidia 的 CUDA 已有近二十年积累和数百万开发者；华为的 CANN 于 2025 年 8 月开源，通过适配层运行现有代码，每个版本都慢半拍。（厂商声称“CUDA 兼容性超过 95%”，未经证实——不要依赖。）CUDA 的吸引力，也是为什么像 RTX PRO 5500 这样的游戏级显卡，价格与昇腾 950PR 相近，却仍然拿到了数以百万计的订单。

这一切加起来意味着什么

账算来算去还是那样。需求约 420 万，合法供应约 260 万，缺口约 160 万——无论是获许可的 H200 细流，还是缺内存的国产产线，2026 年都补不上。价格翻倍。Nvidia 减值数十亿美元，对中国业务不作任何预测。买家明知拿不全，依然下单六百万颗芯片。

这么大、这么赚钱的缺口不会安安静静地待着。它会把芯片从侧门拉进来——灰色市场的服务器、虚假的最终用户声明、转运枢纽。这股拉力，以及被卷入其中的人的下场，就是下一章的内容。
