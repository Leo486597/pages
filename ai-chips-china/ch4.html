Two lines decide where each Nvidia chip sits

On 14 September 2026, Nvidia launched the RTX PRO 5500, a workstation card that the press placed "just below the export control red line". That phrase is the whole chapter. There is a line, chips are built to sit under it, and whether a chip clears it decides who may buy it. In fact there are two lines, and together they sort every Nvidia data-center and gaming chip into one of three fates for China.

Three terms carry the rest of this chapter. A license is written approval from the US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) to ship a named item to a named buyer. An ECCN (Export Control Classification Number) is the code that says which destinations need one; 3A090 is the code for advanced AI chips, 4A090 for computers built with them. TPP (Total Processing Performance) is the single score the US uses to rank a chip's raw power: its dense operations per second times the size in bits of the numbers it handles (our legal team; computelaw.blog). "Dense" matters because Nvidia's headline speed usually assumes half the numbers are zero and skipped, a trick called sparsity that real models rarely get; every TPP here uses the dense rate, half the headline (our chip notes).

The two lines

Line 1 is the license line: TPP 4,800. A chip at or above it is controlled as ECCN 3A090 and needs a license for China, Hong Kong, and Macau, and for any company headquartered in China or Macau wherever that company sits (88 FR 73458, 17 Oct 2023; BIS guidance, 31 May 2026). Under the line, an ordinary buyer needs no US license, though the bans on Entity List parties, military users, and weapons programs still apply. The line has a second net for data-center chips: a dense, efficient chip with TPP as low as 2,400 is caught if it packs enough compute into a small die, so a maker cannot simply shave peak TPP to escape (88 FR 73458). A separate rule since December 2024 controls high-bandwidth memory (HBM) itself, the fast stacked memory that feeds an AI chip, because China cannot yet make it at scale (89 FR 96790, 2 Dec 2024).

Line 2 is the H200 line: TPP under 21,000 and memory bandwidth under 6,500 GB/s. Memory bandwidth is how fast a chip reads its own memory, in gigabytes per second. A controlled chip that stays under both numbers gets case-by-case review, meaning BIS weighs each application instead of starting from "no" (91 FR 1684, effective 15 Jan 2026). A chip over either number faces a presumption of denial: BIS starts from "no". Bandwidth settles most current chips on its own. An 8 TB/s part fails line 2; the H200 at 4.8 TB/s and AMD's MI325X at 6.0 TB/s pass.

One catch runs under everything below: line 2's case-by-case path applies only when the chip ships from the US to an end user in China or Macau. Resale into China from a third country, resale inside China, and sale to a China-headquartered firm abroad all keep the presumption of denial (91 FR 1684).

Two entries break the pattern, and both teach the same lesson: the formula is not the last word.

The H20 sits far under line 1, at TPP 2,368, yet it needs a license. Nvidia built it after the 2023 rule to fit below the line, cutting compute to about one-seventh of an H100 while keeping 96 GB of fast memory and the NVLink chip-to-chip link (our chip notes; Tom's Hardware). In April 2025 BIS sent Nvidia an "is-informed" letter, a private notice that a product needs a license, covering the H20 and "any other circuits achieving the H20's memory bandwidth, interconnect bandwidth, or combination thereof" (NVDA 10-Q, 26 Aug 2026). The letter is not public; its exact scope is known only through Nvidia's own filings. So a chip's sub-threshold specs do not keep it free: BIS can pull any product under control overnight, and the H20 is the proof.

The B30A is the opposite case. It is a cut-down Blackwell, one B300 die instead of two, with about 144 GB of HBM3e and 4 TB/s of bandwidth, reported but never published by Nvidia (Reuters, 19 Aug 2025). Its FP4 math sets its TPP at roughly 28,000, above line 2. The White House blocked it around November 2025, and the September 2026 Xi–Trump summit left that decision standing (Korea Times, 27 Sep 2026). No classification question ever arose, because it was never approved.

Three more flags belong on this table. The RTX PRO 5500 and RTX PRO 6000D have no confirmed US ECCN; their uncontrolled status rests on press reports and the maker's reading, not a BIS writing (our chip notes). The "inferred" classifications in the research files are the team's application of the published thresholds to published specs, not an official ruling. And several China SKUs, from the RTX 4090D to the RTX PRO 6000D, were each built to clear whichever line the last rule drew, so the list of "safe" parts shifts every time a rule changes.

What each chip can actually do

The thresholds sort chips by a compliance score, not by what the chip is good at. Those are different questions, and a buyer needs both.

An AI chip has three strengths, and the job decides which ones matter (our chip FAQ). Training, teaching a model from data, needs all three: high compute, big fast memory, and a fast link between thousands of chips. Only HBM chips with NVLink do it well, so the real training chips are the B300, B200, H200, H100, and A100. Serving a finished model to users, the industry word is inference, needs memory size and memory speed first. A model writes an answer one word piece at a time, and for each piece it reads every weight from memory and does little math, so memory bandwidth, not raw compute, sets the speed. The H20 is the clearest case: 1/15 of a B200's compute, but half its bandwidth, and it sold in huge numbers for serving (our chip notes).

The chip-to-chip link decides how many GPUs can work as one. Nvidia's private NVLink runs at 900 GB/s to 1.8 TB/s per GPU. The standard PCIe slot in any PC runs at 64 to 128 GB/s, roughly ten to twenty times slower (our chip notes). A 70-billion-parameter model needs about 140 GB for its weights alone, more than one chip holds, so it is split across several GPUs that swap partial results many times per word. Over NVLink that is cheap; over PCIe it is the bottleneck. This is why the H200 and H100, with HBM and NVLink, train models that the RTX PRO 5500 cannot, even though the workstation card has more memory per card.

So the cards under line 1 are not weak everywhere. The RTX PRO 5500 carries 84 GB of memory and splits into two isolated 42 GB halves, enough to serve a 70-billion-parameter model on one card (Tom's Hardware, 14 Sep 2026). What it lacks is NVLink, so it scales badly past a handful of cards. It is a fine serving chip and a poor training chip, and that is exactly the slice of the market the US lines leave open to China.

What this means for anyone who is not Nvidia

Two details in line 2 shut the door on a middleman. First, the case-by-case path covers only exports from the US, so a Taiwan-made H200 must enter the US, pass an independent lab test, and pay a 25% tariff before it can go to China (Proclamation 11002, 15 Jan 2026). Second, shipments to China are capped at 50% of the same product's US shipments, a figure only the chip maker holds (91 FR 1684). In our legal team's reading, those two conditions mean only Nvidia or AMD can apply in practice. A third party joins the H200 route only as the named Chinese end user or as a US distributor under the maker's license.

For the chips under line 1, the lawful move is also the plain one: an ordinary buyer needs no US license, but our legal team advises getting the maker's ECCN in writing before relying on "uncontrolled," because BIS can catch any product with a letter, as it did the H20 (our legal team).

Where this goes next

The lines decide what is legal. They do not decide what China wants or how much it can get. The next chapter turns to the gap between the two: more than 3 million orders for the RTX PRO 5500, against a supply Nvidia says can fill 5 to 10 percent, and a 2026 shortfall of roughly 1.6 million chips.

两条线决定每款 Nvidia 芯片的归属

2026 年 9 月 14 日，Nvidia 发布了 RTX PRO 5500。这是一款工作站显卡，媒体报道称它“刚好位于出口管制红线之下”。这句话就是本章的全部要义：有一条线，芯片专门设计成落在线下，而芯片是否越线，决定了谁可以购买。实际上线有两条，二者合起来把 Nvidia 的每一款数据中心芯片和游戏芯片，在中国市场归入三种命运之一。

本章其余部分依靠三个术语。许可证是美国商务部工业与安全局（BIS）出具的书面批准，允许把指定物品运给指定买家。出口管制分类编码（ECCN）用来说明哪些目的地需要许可证；3A090 是先进 AI 芯片的编码，4A090 是搭载这类芯片的计算机的编码。总处理性能（TPP）是美国用来衡量芯片原始算力的单一分数：每秒稠密运算次数乘以所处理数字的位宽（本站法律研究；computelaw.blog）。“稠密”之所以重要，是因为 Nvidia 宣传的峰值速度通常假设一半数字为零并被跳过，这种技巧叫稀疏化，真实模型很少能用上；本章所有 TPP 都按稠密速率计算，即宣传值的一半（本站芯片笔记）。

两条线

第 1 条线是许可证线：TPP 4,800。 达到或超过这条线的芯片被归为 ECCN 3A090，出口到中国大陆、香港和澳门需要许可证；卖给总部位于中国大陆或澳门的公司也需要，无论该公司身在何处（88 FR 73458，2023 年 10 月 17 日；BIS 指引，2026 年 5 月 31 日）。线下的芯片，普通买家无需美国许可证，但对实体清单方、军事用户和武器项目的禁令依然适用。这条线对数据中心芯片还有第二张网：一款稠密、高效的芯片，即使 TPP 低至 2,400，只要在小芯片面积上集中了足够算力，也会被纳入管制，因此厂商无法靠简单压低峰值 TPP 来规避（88 FR 73458）。另有一条规则自 2024 年 12 月起单独管制高带宽内存（HBM）本身，即为 AI 芯片供数的高速堆叠内存，因为中国尚不能大规模生产（89 FR 96790，2024 年 12 月 2 日）。

第 2 条线是 H200 线：TPP 低于 21,000，且内存带宽低于 6,500 GB/s。 内存带宽指芯片读取自身内存的速度，单位是每秒千兆字节。受管制芯片若同时低于这两个数值，适用逐案审查，即 BIS 对每份申请逐一权衡，而不是从“不批”出发（91 FR 1684，2026 年 1 月 15 日生效）。超过任一数值的芯片适用推定拒绝：BIS 从“不批”出发。仅凭带宽就能判定当前大多数芯片。8 TB/s 的产品过不了第 2 条线；H200 为 4.8 TB/s，AMD 的 MI325X 为 6.0 TB/s，都能通过。

下文的一切都受一个限制约束：第 2 条线的逐案审查通道，只适用于芯片从美国运往中国大陆或澳门最终用户的情形。从第三国转售进中国、在中国境内转售、在境外卖给总部位于中国的企业，都仍适用推定拒绝（91 FR 1684）。

有两项打破了规律，二者说明的是同一个道理：公式并非最终定论。

H20 远低于第 1 条线，TPP 只有 2,368，却仍需许可证。Nvidia 在 2023 年规则出台后专门设计它以落在线下，把算力削减到 H100 的约七分之一，同时保留 96 GB 高速内存和 NVLink 芯片互连（本站芯片笔记；Tom's Hardware）。2025 年 4 月，BIS 向 Nvidia 发出“告知函”，即通知某产品需要许可证的非公开函件，覆盖 H20 以及“任何达到 H20 内存带宽、互连带宽或二者组合的其他电路”（NVDA 10-Q，2026 年 8 月 26 日）。该函未公开，其确切范围只能从 Nvidia 自己的披露文件中得知。因此，芯片规格低于门槛并不能保证它不受管制：BIS 可以在一夜之间把任何产品纳入管制，H20 就是证明。

B30A 则是相反的例子。它是缩减版 Blackwell，只有一颗 B300 芯片裸片而非两颗，约 144 GB HBM3e，带宽 4 TB/s；这些规格来自媒体报道，Nvidia 从未公布（Reuters，2025 年 8 月 19 日）。按其 FP4 运算计，TPP 约为 28,000，超过第 2 条线。白宫约在 2025 年 11 月拦下了它，2026 年 9 月的习特峰会也维持了这一决定（Korea Times，2026 年 9 月 27 日）。它从未获批，所以分类问题根本没有出现过。

这张表还需要另外三点提示。RTX PRO 5500 和 RTX PRO 6000D 没有经确认的美国 ECCN；它们“不受管制”的地位依据的是媒体报道和厂商自己的解读，而非 BIS 的书面文件（本站芯片笔记）。研究文件中的“推断”分类，是团队把已公布的门槛套用到已公布的规格上得出的，并非官方认定。此外，从 RTX 4090D 到 RTX PRO 6000D，多款中国特供型号都是为了越过上一次规则划出的线而设计的，所以每当规则变化，“安全”型号的名单就会随之改变。

每款芯片实际能做什么

门槛按合规分数给芯片排序，而不是按芯片擅长什么排序。这是两个不同的问题，买家两者都需要了解。

AI 芯片有三项能力，具体用途决定哪项最重要（本站芯片笔记 FAQ）。训练，即用数据教会模型，三项都需要：高算力、大容量高速内存，以及数千颗芯片之间的高速互连。只有配备 NVLink 的 HBM 芯片才能胜任，所以真正的训练芯片是 B300、B200、H200、H100 和 A100。服务，即把训练好的模型提供给用户，业内称为推理，首先需要内存容量和内存速度。模型生成答案时一次只写出一个词元，每写一个都要从内存读取全部权重，而计算量很小，因此决定速度的是内存带宽，而不是原始算力。H20 是最明显的例子：算力只有 B200 的 1/15，带宽却有其一半，作为推理芯片销量巨大（本站芯片笔记）。

芯片互连决定多少块 GPU 能协同如一。Nvidia 专有的 NVLink 每块 GPU 速率为 900 GB/s 到 1.8 TB/s。任何 PC 上的标准 PCIe 插槽速率为 64 到 128 GB/s，大约慢十到二十倍（本站芯片笔记）。一个 700 亿参数的模型仅权重就需要约 140 GB，超过单颗芯片的容量，因此要拆分到多块 GPU 上，每生成一个词都要多次交换中间结果。走 NVLink 代价很小；走 PCIe 就成了瓶颈。这就是为什么配备 HBM 和 NVLink 的 H200、H100 能训练 RTX PRO 5500 训练不了的模型，尽管这款工作站卡的单卡内存更大。

所以第 1 条线以下的卡并非处处都弱。RTX PRO 5500 配有 84 GB 内存，可拆分为两个相互隔离的 42 GB 部分，单卡就足以运行一个 700 亿参数模型的推理服务（Tom's Hardware，2026 年 9 月 14 日）。它缺的是 NVLink，因此超过少数几块卡后扩展性很差。它是出色的推理芯片、糟糕的训练芯片，而这恰恰是美国这两条线留给中国的那块市场。

这对 Nvidia 以外的人意味着什么

第 2 条线中的两个细节，把中间商挡在了门外。第一，逐案审查通道只覆盖从美国出口的情形，所以一颗台湾制造的 H200 必须先进入美国，通过独立实验室测试，并缴纳 25% 关税，才能运往中国（Proclamation 11002，2026 年 1 月 15 日）。第二，对华出货量上限为同一产品美国出货量的 50%，而这个数字只有芯片厂商掌握（91 FR 1684）。按本站法律研究的解读，这两个条件意味着实际上只有 Nvidia 或 AMD 能够申请。第三方要参与 H200 通道，只能作为具名的中国最终用户，或作为在厂商许可证下运作的美国分销商。

对于第 1 条线以下的芯片，合法做法也很简单：普通买家无需美国许可证，但本站法律研究建议在依赖“不受管制”之前，先取得厂商书面确认的 ECCN，因为 BIS 可以用一封函件把任何产品纳入管制，就像对 H20 那样（本站法律研究）。

接下来

这两条线决定什么是合法的，却不决定中国想要什么、能得到多少。下一章讨论这两者之间的缺口：RTX PRO 5500 的订单超过 300 万块，而 Nvidia 表示供应只能满足 5% 到 10%，2026 年的缺口约为 160 万颗芯片。
